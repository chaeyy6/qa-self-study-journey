# Scrum & Agile Practices

## Learning Objective

Understand the fundamentals of Scrum and how Agile teams use Sprints, Scrum events, and progress-tracking practices to plan, develop, test, and continuously improve software.

## My Understanding

Scrum is an Agile framework that organizes software development into short, fixed-length iterations called Sprints. During each Sprint, the team works on selected Product Backlog items and aims to produce a usable product Increment.

The Scrum Team includes the Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers. They collaborate throughout the Sprint rather than treating development and testing as completely separate activities.

Scrum also provides events such as Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective to help teams plan their work, monitor progress, receive feedback, and improve their processes.

---

## QA Perspective

As a tester in a Scrum Team, testing is performed throughout the Sprint rather than being left until the end.

A QA Engineer may contribute by:

- Reviewing User Stories and Acceptance Criteria.
- Identifying risks and test conditions.
- Participating in Sprint Planning and Daily Scrums.
- Testing completed functionality during the Sprint.
- Reporting and discussing defects with developers.
- Verifying that completed work satisfies the Acceptance Criteria and Definition of Done.
- Providing feedback during Sprint Reviews.
- Contributing to process improvements during Retrospectives.

Tools such as Burndown Charts and Velocity can also help the team understand progress and plan future work.

---

## Real-World Examples

- A team works on a login feature during a two-week Sprint.
- The Product Owner prioritizes a payment bug in the Product Backlog.
- Developers and testers discuss testing risks during the Daily Scrum.
- A Burndown Chart shows how much Sprint work remains.
- The team's previous Velocity helps estimate how much work it can reasonably take into the next Sprint.
- During the Retrospective, the team decides to involve testers earlier in feature development.

## Key Takeaways

- Scrum is an Agile framework that organizes work into fixed-length Sprints.
- The Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers have different responsibilities within the Scrum Team.
- Scrum events help teams plan, coordinate, review results, and improve their way of working.
- The Product Backlog contains product work, while the Sprint Backlog contains work selected for the current Sprint.
- Acceptance Criteria apply to a specific User Story, while the Definition of Done defines the team's standard for completed work.
- Burndown Charts show remaining work, while Burnup Charts show completed work and can make scope changes easier to see.
- Velocity uses historical team performance to help forecast future capacity.
- Sprint Retrospectives help the team identify and implement process improvements.
- Testing is integrated throughout the Sprint and is part of the team's overall responsibility for quality.
