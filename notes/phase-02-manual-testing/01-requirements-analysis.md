# Requirements Analysis

## Overview

Requirements Analysis is the first practical activity performed by a QA Engineer during the Software Testing Life Cycle (STLC).

Before developers begin implementing a feature, QA reviews the software requirements to ensure they are complete, clear, consistent, and testable. The goal is to identify ambiguities, missing information, conflicting requirements, and potential risks as early as possible.

Requirements Analysis is a form of **Static Testing** because no software is executed. Instead, QA evaluates the requirements documentation to prevent defects before development begins.

---

After completing this lesson, I should be able to:

- Understand the purpose of Requirements Analysis.
- Identify ambiguous and incomplete requirements.
- Recognize missing business rules and acceptance criteria.
- Ask clarification questions before development starts.
- Understand how early requirement reviews reduce software defects and project costs.

## Topics Covered

### Shopping Cart Requirements

Learn how to distinguish between poorly written and well-defined requirements by identifying missing details such as:

- Pricing information
- Quantity limits
- Confirmation behavior
- Tax calculations

---

### Checkout Flow Requirements

Analyze checkout requirements and determine whether they clearly define:

- Login behavior
- Payment processing
- Error handling
- Confirmation emails
- Processing cut-off times

---

### Discounts & Coupon Requirements

Review coupon-related requirements to determine whether they clearly specify:

- Coupon application rules
- Multiple coupon behavior
- Error messages
- Tax calculation order

## Importance of Requirements Analysis

Poor requirements often lead to:

- Misunderstood features
- Incorrect implementations
- Increased development costs
- More production defects
- Rework for both developers and testers

Well-written requirements help everyone involved in the project understand exactly what needs to be built and tested.

As a QA Engineer, my responsibility is **not to blindly accept the requirements**. Instead, I should analyze them, identify gaps, and collaborate with stakeholders to improve their quality before development begins.

---

## Outcome

By completing this lesson, I understand that software quality starts long before writing test cases or executing software.

Requirements Analysis allows QA Engineers to prevent defects rather than simply finding them later. Clear and complete requirements lead to better development, more effective testing, fewer misunderstandings, and higher-quality software.
